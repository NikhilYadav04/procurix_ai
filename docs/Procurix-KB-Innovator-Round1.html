Target users

The person who is the procurement department

Primary

The SME buyer

₹5 Cr – ₹500 Cr turnover. Construction, manufacturing, facilities, hospitality. One or two people, often the founder or a site engineer. No procurement software — Ariba and Coupa are built and priced for enterprises.

Benefits without using it

The MSME supplier

Never logs in. Replies to email, or opens an auction link. Gets paid on time because their buyer can finally see the deadline.

They buySteel, furniture, fasteners, safety gear, site consumables — repeat categories, not one-offs
How oftenSeveral orders a week, every week
Suppliers20 to 100 on the books, most of them small local firms — so MSME exposure is the norm, not the exception
TodayEmail, Excel, and one person's memory
Why it must be agentic

Three properties defeat a form, a workflow engine, and a chatbot

1

You don't control the input

Quotes arrive as scans and photographs. Portals fix this by making the supplier fill a form first — which is exactly why they never reach Indian SMEs. An agent reads whatever arrives. The mess is the reason it must be a model.

2

It's a chain, not a transaction

One sentence expands into eight steps spread over days. A revised price, a missing delivery date, a vendor replying late — the branches are not a closed set, so they cannot be declared in advance.

3

The criteria change every time

Cheapest is not best. This month delivery decides it, next month payment terms. And always: is this supplier MSME — because that sets when you are legally obliged to pay.

Not for: large enterprises already running a procure-to-pay suite, government tendering, or consumer buying. The wedge is firms whose statutory obligation is identical to an enterprise's but who have no system at all.

The exposure nobody sees until it is permanent

₹4,60,000 Tax falling due this year on one unpaid ₹18.40 L invoice to a micro supplier, past its 30-day term — plus interest at 3× the bank rate, compounded, non-deductible.
The remedy costs nothing. Pay on day 44 instead of day 46. The entire value is that somebody knew.

43B(h) binds every Indian buyer with MSME suppliers from FY 2023–24. Enterprises absorbed it through existing systems. The firms most exposed absorbed nothing, because nothing was built for them.

What we have not solved: we do not discover suppliers — vendors are added by the buyer. GSTIN structure and check digits are validated offline, but live registry verification is not yet wired, so MSME status is recorded rather than proven, and the interface says so. Nothing runs on a schedule yet; a background watcher on the inbox and the payment clock is next.
